NASA Roman Wide-Field Science Team

Kinematic Lensing with the Roman Space Telescope

We are building the pipeline to measure cosmic shear one galaxy at a time — combining Roman’s imaging with its slitless spectroscopy to cut the shape noise that limits weak lensing by about an order of magnitude.

How kinematic lensing works Publications Code on GitHub

Why it matters

Breaking the shape–shear degeneracy

Weak lensing distorts galaxy shapes by about one percent, while galaxies’ intrinsic shapes scatter by tens of percent. Standard weak lensing beats this “shape noise” down by averaging huge numbers of faint galaxies. Kinematic lensing instead uses each disk galaxy’s rotation to infer its intrinsic shape directly.

~10×
Lower shape noise

Simulated analyses give KL shape noise of σε ≈ 0.02–0.04, versus ≈ 0.26 for standard weak lensing.

>2×
Dark-energy figure of merit

For the Roman High Latitude Wide Area Survey Medium Tier, KL alone more than doubles the figure of merit of standard weak lensing.

3
Galaxies for a cluster detection

Our pilot measurement detected the lensing signal of Abell 2261 using only three background disk galaxies.

Our program

From Roman pixels to cosmology

The team is building an end-to-end kinematic lensing analysis for Roman’s High Latitude Wide Area Survey.

01 · Simulate

Realistic grism mocks

Simulated Roman slitless (grism) observations of rotating disk galaxies with varied morphologies, including mocks built from hydrodynamical simulations.

02 · Measure

Per-galaxy shear inference

A Roman-specific pipeline that jointly forward-models imaging and spectroscopy to turn each galaxy into a shear estimate — a KL shape catalog.

03 · Interpret

Cosmological inference

Cosmology software built on CosmoLike, combining KL with galaxy clustering and galaxy–galaxy lensing, with systematics modeled throughout.

04 · Plan

Survey forecasts

Forecasts of KL performance across survey strategies — tiers, depths and roll angles — and levels of systematics control.
